\paragraph{Stemmed perfect binary trees.}
Fix $m, n \in \natz$.
Write $[m] \coloneq \{1, 2, \dots, m\}$ and, as usual,
$\bit^{\le n} \coloneq \bigcup_{0 \le i \le n} \bit^i$,
with $\emptyword$ denoting the empty word.

A \emph{stemmed perfect binary tree of stem length $m$ and (binary) height $n$} over a nonempty set $\Sigma$ is a function
\[
T \colon [m] \uplus \bit^{\le n} \to \Sigma .
\]
The elements of $[m]$ are the \emph{stem nodes} and the elements of $\bit^{\le n}$ are the \emph{binary nodes}. The \emph{root} of $T$ is the stem node $1$ if $m \ge 1$ and the binary node $\emptyword$ otherwise. The parent-child relation is fixed by:
\begin{itemize}
    \item every stem node $i \in [m-1]$ has the unique child $i + 1$, also a stem node;
    \item the deepest stem node $m$ (when $m \ge 1$) has the unique child $\emptyword$, the root of the binary part;
    \item every internal binary node $v \in \bit^{<n}$ has children $v.0$ and $v.1$;
    \item every binary leaf $v \in \bit^n$ has no children.
\end{itemize}
The \emph{height} of a node is its distance from the root:
\[
\htof{v} \coloneq
\begin{cases}
i-1 & \text{if } v = i \in [m], \\
m + |v| & \text{if } v \in \bit^{\le n}.
\end{cases}
\]
We call $\htof{v}$ also the \emph{level} of~$v$.
Levels $0,\dots,m-1$ form the \emph{stem levels} and levels $m,\dots,m+n$ form the \emph{binary levels}; the binary leaves all sit at level $m+n$.
We refer to $T$ as a \emph{stemmed tree} of \emph{shape} $(m,n)$; when $m=0$ a stemmed tree is just a perfect binary tree in the sense of~\cite{LSTA2025}.

In this work, stemmed trees of shape $(m,n)$ serve as semantic objects representing
pairs $(\sigma, \varphi)$ where
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\sigma \colon X \to \bools$ is a \emph{classical store} over a fixed finite set
      of $m$ Boolean variables $X = \{x_1, \dots, x_m\}$, obtained by reading off
      the Boolean value carried by the label at each stem node; and
    \item $\varphi \in \complex^{2^n}$ is the \emph{pure $n$-qubit state} whose
      computational-basis amplitude at $v \in \bit^n$ is $T(v)$.
\end{itemize}
Internal binary nodes carry no amplitudes; they encode only the binary branching structure of the quantum part. Henceforth, \emph{tree} means stemmed perfect binary tree.

\paragraph{Automation}
We consider the \emph{stemmed level-synchronized tree automation (\slsta)}, an \lsta of a form specialized to stemmed trees defined above. 
Let $\Sigma$ be a ranked alphabet with $\#: \Sigma \to \{0,1,2\}$, where symbols of rank~0 label leaves, symbols of rank~{1} label the ``classical'' internal nodes and symbols of rank~{2} label ``quantum'' internal nodes.
We denote the set of rank~0 symbols by $\Sigma_0$ and the set of rank~1 symbols by $\mathtt{Store}$. 
An \slsta is a tuple $\aut = \tuple{Q,\Sigma,\Delta,\rootstates}$ where $Q$ is a finite set of \emph{states}, 
$\rootstates \subseteq Q$ is a set of \emph{root states}, and $\Delta$ is a finite set of transitions of the following forms:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
    \transtree{q}{C}{f(q_0,q_1)}  &\text{internal binary transition}  \\
    \transtree{q}{B}{b(p)} &\text{internal unary transition}\\
    \transtree{q}{C}{f} & \text{leaf transition} \\
\end{array}
\]
with $q_0,q_1,p \in Q$, $f \in \Sigma$ and $B, C \subseteq \natz$ a finite set of \emph{choices}. 
For a transition $\delta \in \Delta$, we write $\topof\delta$ for its source state, $\symof\delta$ for its symbol, 
$\ell(\delta)$ for its choice set,
and $\botof\delta$ for its pair of target states (in the binary case).
Transitions with the same source state have pairwise disjoint choice sets, i.e., 
\[
\forall \delta_1 \neq \delta_2 \in \Delta: \topof{\delta_1} = \topof{\delta_2} \implies \ell(\delta_1) \cap \ell(\delta_2) = \emptyset.
\]

$v \in [m-1]$, $\botof\run(v) = \bigl(v+1 \bigr)$;
$v = m$, $\botof\run(v) = \bigl( \run(0) \big)$;
$v \in \bit^{\le n}$,

$\botof{\run(v)}=\bigl(\topof{\run(v.0)}, \topof{\run(v.1)}\bigr)$; while $v$ is a leaf node, $\run(v)$ is of the form $\transtree{q}{C}{T(v)}$.

For each \emph{level $d$}, we define %\jal{level notation is not used anywhere, so if necessary we can remove it and use length of node to say level anywhere it concerns.}
$\level(\run,d) \coloneq \{ \run(w) \mid w \in \dom(T) \land \htof{w} = d\}$.
A run is \emph{level-synchronized} if all transitions from the same level share at least one common choice, or equivalently, for all $d \in \natz$, 
\[C_d \coloneq \bigcap_{\delta \in \level(\run,d)} \ell(\delta) \neq \emptyset.\] 
An \emph{accepting} run is a run $\run$ such that $\topof{\run(\emptyword)} \in \rootstates$ and $\run$ is level-synchronized.

\paragraph{Classical Unary Boolean Operations}
We consider $\texttt{SET\ d,v}$ and $\texttt{NOT\ d}$ for $1 \leq d \leq m$ on the stem. The corresponding \slsta transformations are straightforward:
\begin{itemize}
\item for $\texttt{SET\ d,v}$, modify the transitions $\transtree{p}{C}{b(q)} \in \Delta_k$ by $\transtree{p}{C}{v(q)}$
\item for $\texttt{NOT\ d}$, modify the transitions $\transtree{p}{C}{b(q)} \in \Delta_k$ by $\transtree{p}{C}{\overline{b}(q)}$
\end{itemize} 

\paragraph{Classical (Downward) Binary operations}
Let $f: \bit^2 \to \bit$ be a Boolean function. Fix $1 \leq s < d \leq m$. We consider the following classical binary instructions only:
\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\hline
Instruction & Meaning & \(f(u,v)\) \\
\hline

\texttt{COPY\ s,d} & \(y_d:=y_s\) & \(u\) \\
\texttt{AND\ s,d} & \(y_d:=y_s\land y_d\) & \(u\land v\) \\
\texttt{OR\ s,d} & \(y_d:=y_s\lor y_d\) & \(u\lor v\) \\
\texttt{XOR\ s,d} & \(y_d:=y_s\oplus y_d\) & \(u\oplus v\) \\
%\bottomrule
\hline 
\end{tabular}\end{center}
We construct the corresponding sLSTA transformations for the above binary instructions associated to a Boolean function $f$ as follows.
Let $M_{s,d} = \biguplus_{k=s+1}^d P_k$. All the states in $M_{s,d}$ are replaced as
\begin{equation*}
P'_k = \begin{cases}
\set{[p,u] \mid p \in P_k\  \land\ u \in \bit } &\text{ if } s < k \leq d \\
P_k &\text{ otherwise}
\end{cases}, \quad Q'_q =Q_q, \quad R'=R, \quad \Phi' = \Phi.
\end{equation*}
Transitions are all preserved excepts on the stem interval between $s$ and $d$.
\begin{itemize}
\item (read the source): at $k=s$, 
\begin{equation*}
\Delta'_k := \set{\transtree{p}{C}{b[q,b]} \mid  \transtree{p}{C}{b(q)} \in \Delta_k}
\end{equation*}
\item (carry the bit through intervening positions): if $s < k < d$, for $u \in \bit$, each
\begin{equation*}
\Delta'_k := \set{ \transtree{[p,u]}{C}{b([q,u])} \mid [p,u] \in P'_k\ \land\ \transtree{p}{C}{b(q)} \in \Delta_k }
\end{equation*}
\item (write): if $k=d$,
\begin{equation*}
\Delta'_k:= \set{ \transtree{[p,u]}{C}{f(u,b)(q)} \mid [p,u] \in P'_k \ \land \ \transtree{p}{C}{b(q)} \in \Delta_k}
\end{equation*}
\end{itemize}

Soundness and complexity: 
    \begin{itemize}
        \item The constructions of the classical operations are correct.
        \item $|Q'| = |Q| + |M|, \quad |\Delta'| \leq |\Delta| + |D|$ where $M= M_{s,d}$ and $D= \biguplus_{k=s+1}^d \Delta_d$.
    \end{itemize}

In the context of verifying classical-quantum (CQ) programs, a critical challenge is faithfully representing the global state of the system while respecting the fundamental physical and mathematical differences between classical and quantum information. While Level-Synchronized Tree Automata (LSTA) provide a synchronized structure for quantum states, forcing classical variables into a symmetric automata framework introduces artificial dependencies and over-engineering.

To address this, we decouple the classical state from the automata structure entirely, proposing a Choice-Indexed Valuation Map (CIVM). This approach is built upon three core observations:

1. The Ontological Divergence of State Representations

The mathematical foundations of classical and quantum variables are inherently different.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Quantum states (Tensor Product):} Due to superposition and entanglement, a quantum system resides in a joint Hilbert space ($\mathcal{H}_1 \otimes \mathcal{H}_2 \cdots \otimes \mathcal{H}_n$). The state of a single qubit cannot be independently evaluated without considering its correlation with the entire system. 
\end{itemize}
